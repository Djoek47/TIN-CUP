# TIN CUP — Product Bible (Stage 1)

**Working title:** Tin Cup (from the beggar's tin cup — short, memeable, ownable, already the name of your draft)
**One-liner:** The Wild West arena where strangers throw real money at strangers — for glory, for laughs, for the story.

---

## STEP 1 — Understanding the Product

**Core concept, rewritten.** Tin Cup is not a charity app, a fintech app, or a gambling app. It is a *live entertainment economy*. The content is money moving between strangers in public. Vagrants perform for money. Lords perform generosity. The audience watches both perform. The transaction is the show.

The nearest true comparison is not GoFundMe — it's TikTok LIVE gifting with the camera turned around: instead of gifting a creator for content, the *ask itself* is the content. Begging becomes a performance genre, the way roasting became a genre.

**Why it exists.** Three internet behaviors already exist at massive scale and have no dedicated home: (1) public e-begging (Twitter "sugar daddy" culture, Cash App drops, "$5 if you..."), (2) performative generosity (MrBeast built the biggest channel on Earth on "I gave a stranger $10,000"), and (3) chaos-watching (Kick, Fear&Greed slop streams). Tin Cup fuses all three into one arena and takes a cut of every transaction.

**Emotional value.**
- For Lords: status, power, the god-mode feeling of changing a stranger's day with a tap, in front of an audience.
- For Vagrants: hope, the lottery-ticket thrill, and the dignity loophole — begging inside a satirical Wild West costume is *play*, not humiliation. The character absorbs the shame.
- For Observers: schadenfreude, awe, comedy, parasocial rooting.

**Entertainment value.** Every beg is a micro-drama with real stakes. Real money makes stakes real; the Wild West skin makes it safe to laugh.

**Market positioning.** "The most ridiculous app on the internet." Sits between Kick (chaos), TikTok LIVE (gifting), and MrBeast (generosity-as-content). No direct competitor owns "strangers giving strangers money as entertainment."

**Why users care.** It's free money (Vagrants), it's godhood (Lords), it's the best reality show on your phone (Observers).

**Why investors care.** Take-rate on GMV (~11% blended), creator-economy dynamics without content production costs (users generate everything), whale-driven revenue concentration like live-streaming apps (top 1% of TikTok LIVE gifters drive a huge share of gifting revenue), and a brand that markets itself through outrage and memes.

**The honest version, because you asked for honesty:** the three things most likely to kill this are (1) app store distribution + payments regulation, (2) the optics of monetizing desperation, and (3) real-money gambling mechanics triggering gaming licenses. This bible restructures the concept to survive all three without losing the HELL YEAH. The changes are flagged inline as **[STRATEGIC CHANGE]**.

## STEP 2 — Product Philosophy

Twelve pillars, and what carries each:

- **Status** — Titles (Lord → Baron → Tycoon → Monarch's Court), public gifting animations, leaderboards. Status is the #1 monetization driver; people don't pay $500 to give $500, they pay $500 to be *seen* giving $500.
- **Chaos** — The Feed of Madness, absurd challenges, Beggar's Roulette. Chaos is the acquisition engine; clips of chaos are what gets screen-recorded onto TikTok.
- **Comedy** — The satirical Wild West frame. Comedy is the legal and moral shield: it reframes begging as bit, not desperation.
- **Hope** — The Vagrant's slot machine: "my beg might hit today." Hope drives daily return visits.
- **Luck** — Roulette, Lucky Hours, random Lord drops. Variable-ratio reward is the strongest retention mechanic known to behavioral science.
- **Generosity** — Real dopamine from giving, amplified by audience. Lords must *feel* like heroes, not marks.
- **Recognition** — Every gift triggers public spectacle: name in lights, gunshots, coin rain. Recognition converts one-time givers into repeat givers.
- **Power** — Lords set challenges, run courts, command worshippers. Power asymmetry is the product's core tension — and its core appeal.
- **Community** — Saloons, courts, guilds. Community converts a novelty app into a habit app.
- **Competition** — Leaderboards by season, Lord-vs-Lord generosity duels. Competition between whales is where the real money prints (whale wars are the top revenue event in every live-gifting app).
- **Storytelling** — Every beg is a story; the AI Storyteller and rags-to-riches arcs give the app a narrative spine.
- **Identity** — Your outlaw character. **[STRATEGIC CHANGE]** Do not force "ugly designs" on Vagrants. Instead: *everyone starts in rags.* Cosmetic squalor is the starting state, not a caste marker, and climbing out of it is the progression fantasy. Forced ugliness reads as the platform mocking poor people; chosen rags-to-riches reads as a game. Same visual comedy, opposite optics.

**The master loop that ties all pillars together — [STRATEGIC CHANGE, the single best addition to your concept]: Class Mobility.** A Vagrant who accumulates enough coins can *buy a title and become a Lord.* This one rule transforms the app from a static caste system (legally and morally ugly, and boring) into an aspiration machine: every Vagrant is a future Lord, every beg is a step in a rags-to-riches story, and the most viral possible clip becomes "he begged his way to Baron." It also doubles your Lord supply over time.

## STEP 3 — Competitive Analysis (psychology only)

- **Kalshi / prediction markets** — Being *right in public* is intoxicating; opinion becomes stake. Steal: public commitment + public payoff. (Note: Kalshi is a regulated exchange, not slop — the lesson is that "money + opinion + audience" is the formula, and they pay millions for the license to run it. Respect what that implies.)
- **Rubet / Rainbet / Stake** — Near-miss design, session momentum, VIP ladders, streamer symbiosis (gambling clips ARE the marketing). Steal: the streamer-as-distribution model — your Lords are your Trainwrecks.
- **Robinhood** — Confetti taught the industry that celebration animation drives repeat behavior; frictionless money movement removes the "wallet pain" moment. Steal: make giving feel like winning.
- **TikTok** — Infinite variable-reward feed; the algorithm as slot machine. Steal: Feed of Madness must be algorithmic, full-screen, swipe-based. Non-negotiable.
- **TikTok LIVE Gifts / Twitch Bits** — The genius is *proxy currency* (coins/bits) which decouples spending from dollar-pain, plus *public gift animations* which turn spending into broadcasting. Battles between streamers spike gifting because fans fight wars with wallets. Steal: coin abstraction + gift spectacle + Lord-vs-Lord battles.
- **Discord** — Belonging through servers; roles as identity. Steal: Courts (a Lord's private server) with role hierarchy (Worshipper → Favored → Knight).
- **Kick** — Lax-moderation chaos as brand identity; the platform itself is the edgy character. Steal: brand voice, not moderation policy — you need the Sheriff or Apple removes you.
- **GoFundMe** — Progress bars are hope made visible; social proof ("47 people gave") lowers the giving barrier. Steal: beg goals with progress bars and giver counts.
- **OnlyFans gifting** — Parasocial 1:1 economics; the whale who wants to be *known* by the receiver. Steal: Lords can "sponsor" a specific Vagrant (patron mechanic).
- **Reddit Awards** — People pay real money to decorate *strangers'* content because it's public taste-signaling. Steal: cheap micro-gifts (a "Nickel Toss") so Observers convert into micro-Lords.
- **Fantasy Football** — Season structure creates appointment behavior and trash talk. Steal: Seasons ("Gold Rushes") with resets, drafts of Vagrants ("stake your claim on a Drifter, earn bragging rights when they climb").

Cross-cutting extraction: **variable rewards + public status + proxy currency + season resets + whale wars.** Those five systems are the entire psychological engine. Everything else is skin.

## STEP 4 — User Personas

**"Big Tex" — the Lord/Whale (top 1%, ~60–75% of revenue).** 28–50, disposable income (crypto, business owner, oil-money energy). Motivation: power, worship, being the main character. Frustration: nothing to spend on that *feels big enough*; slow recognition. Spending: bursts of $500–$10k during live events, especially when dueling another Lord. Design mandate: give him escalating spectacle — the $1,000 gift animation must stop the entire lobby.

**"Dusty" — the Vagrant/Grinder.** 18–30, broke or bored, extremely online. Motivation: money, obviously — but stays for the game (levels, roulette, the climb to Lord). Frustration: begging into the void; getting nothing for effort. Spending: minimal; converts coins out, but reinvests some into cosmetics once identity forms. Design mandate: guarantee *some* reward cadence (daily coin dust, roulette entries) so zero-earning days still feel like progress.

**"The Duchess" — the Performer-Vagrant.** A content creator who treats begging as sketch comedy. Motivation: virality first, money second. She is your growth engine — her clips recruit everyone else. Design mandate: one-tap watermark export to TikTok/X/IG; "Most Ridiculous Beg" awards.

**The Observer (90% of users).** Lurks, laughs, shares. Motivation: free entertainment. Conversion path: Nickel Toss micro-gifts → small Lord. Design mandate: never wall content behind signup; guest mode watches everything.

**The Collector.** Buys cosmetics, chases rare badges, completes sets. Low drama, steady revenue. Design mandate: seasonal limited cosmetics with visible scarcity counters.

**The Gambler.** Wants roulette and duels. **[FLAG]** He's real revenue but he's also your license risk — see Step 12/14.

**The Influencer Lord.** Streams his generosity for clout (MrBeast-lite). Frustration: clip tooling. Design mandate: auto-clip his biggest drops.

**The Meme Creator.** Doesn't transact; manufactures the culture. Give him screenshot-perfect moments (beg cards designed to be screenshotted).

**Monarchs (you).** Your dashboard needs: GMV, take, fraud queue, whale health, viral coefficient.

## STEP 5 — Brand Identity

**Mission:** Make generosity the wildest show on the internet.
**Story:** Somewhere past the edge of the polite internet is a lawless town where fortunes change hands in the street. Drifters roll in with nothing but a tin cup and a story. Lords ride through throwing gold. The Sheriff keeps just enough order that nobody actually gets hurt. Everybody's playing a character; the money's real.

**Voice:** Deadpan Western drawl with terminally-online irony. Never sincere-corporate, never mean. Notifications talk like a saloon barker: "A Baron just emptied his purse on your sorry beg. 89 coins, partner."
**Tone spectrum:** 80% comedy, 15% hype, 5% dead-serious (money, safety, KYC — the Sheriff never jokes).

**Color psychology:** Midnight blue-black base (night in the desert; makes gold pop), saturated gold (#F5B32B family — money, reward, every coin moment), oxblood red (danger, Sheriff warnings, high stakes), parchment cream (cards, readability), cactus green reserved *only* for cash-out/received-money moments (learned association: green = you got paid).

**Typography:** A slab/wanted-poster display face for headlines and titles (Western without cliché — think a modernized Clarendon), paired with a clean geometric sans for UI/body (money and numbers must be instantly legible; fintech clarity inside carnival chrome). Numbers get a tabular mono treatment in wallets.

**Illustration & character style:** Chunky, slightly grotesque 2D cartoon — Supercell squash-and-stretch meets Lackadaisy Western. NOT realistic (realism makes begging sad), NOT corporate-flat (kills the chaos). Characters are customizable outlaws: hats, dusters, gold teeth, absurd items (a horse that's clearly two guys in a costume).
**Mascot:** "The Monarch" — a fat, smug vulture in a crown who takes his cut on-screen during transactions ("the vulture takes his taste"). Making the platform's fee a *character* turns your rake into a joke users repeat instead of a hidden fee they resent. Secondary mascot: the Sheriff — a tired, incorruptible armadillo.

**Iconography:** Tin cup (begging), gold coin stack (wallet), wanted poster (profiles), sheriff star (safety), swinging doors (lobbies), dynamite (challenges).
**Animation style:** Overcranked and physical — coin rain with weight and bounce, gunshot muzzle flashes on big gifts, tumbleweed on empty states, screen-shake on $500+ drops. Every animation designed to look good in a vertical screen recording, because screen recordings are your ad campaign.

**World-building / lore:** The town is "Perdition Gulch." Seasons are Gold Rushes. The general lobby is Main Street. Lords' private lobbies are Courts inside their Ranch. The clip feed is the Town Crier / Feed of Madness. Bans are "run out of town." Appeals happen at "the Courthouse." Lore consistency is what makes the app *culturally recognizable* — every screenshot should be identifiable as Tin Cup without a logo.

## STEP 7 — Feature Breakdown (priority-ordered)

**P0 — MVP cannot ship without:**

1. **Beg posts + gifting (the core loop).** Purpose: the product. Psychology: hope + public generosity. Monetization: the rake. Difficulty: medium (payments hard, UI easy). Dependencies: wallet, KYC, Sheriff scan.
2. **Wallet + coin system + KYC + cash-out.** Purpose: money rails. Note: coins abstraction (99 coins = $99) softens spend pain and is industry standard. Difficulty: HIGH — this is 60% of engineering. Use a licensed payments partner (see Step 12).
3. **Sheriff pre-scan (AI moderation on every beg/challenge before publish).** Purpose: app-store survival. Difficulty: medium (third-party moderation APIs + rules).
4. **Feed of Madness (clips) + one-tap watermarked share.** Purpose: growth. A gifting app without a share loop is a dead gifting app. Difficulty: medium.
5. **Profiles (Wanted Posters) + star ratings + trust score.** Purpose: scam prevention + identity. Difficulty: low-medium.
6. **Gift spectacle system (tiered animations by amount).** Purpose: converts Lords into repeat Lords. This is a P0, not polish — the spectacle IS the product for whales. Difficulty: medium.

**P1 — fast-follow (weeks 4–12):**

7. **Lord challenges with escrowed bounties.** Psychology: power + chaos; the most clippable format. Escrow prevents "do the thing, get stiffed." Difficulty: medium.
8. **Live Saloon Streams with real-time tipping + Lord-vs-Lord generosity battles.** Whale-war revenue engine. Difficulty: HIGH (live infra — consider Agora/LiveKit).
9. **Goal begs (progress bars).** Psychology: hope made visible + social proof. Difficulty: low.
10. **Titles ladder + class mobility (Vagrant→Lord buy-in).** The aspiration loop. Difficulty: low.
11. **Nickel Toss micro-gifts (observer conversion).** Difficulty: low, high leverage.
12. **Leaderboards + Season 1 "Gold Rush."** Difficulty: low-medium.

**P2 — retention & monetization depth:**

13. **Courts (Lord private lobbies + roles).** 14. **Cosmetics shop + rags-to-riches outfit progression.** 15. **AI Dramatizer/Storyteller.** 16. **Beggar's Roulette (free-entry, Lord-funded pots — sweepstakes-structured, counsel-approved).** 17. **Monarch Pass subscription.** 18. **Skill duels for cosmetics/bragging (NOT real-money wagers — see Step 12).** 19. **Guilds/Posses.** 20. **Sponsored begs / brand events.**

**Explicitly cut from v1 [STRATEGIC CHANGE]:** real-money peer-to-peer gambling mini-games ("gamble their own coins vs other users"). That single feature converts you from a payments company into an unlicensed casino in most US states. It's the difference between a hard compliance project and an impossible one. Ship the *feeling* of gambling (roulette skins, luck events, duels) with sweepstakes or cosmetic-stakes structures, and revisit real wagering only with gaming counsel and probably a separate licensed entity.

## STEP 8 — User Flows (condensed to decisions and states)

**Guest:** open app → full watch access (feed, lobbies, streams) → any *action* (gift, beg, follow) triggers signup sheet. Never gate watching; the show is the funnel.

**Registration:** phone/Apple/Google → age gate (18+, DOB + later verified at KYC) → pick side (Drifter free / Lord path) → character creator (60 seconds max, skippable) → land on Main Street with a guided first action (Drifters: draft your first beg; Lords: witness a live drop, then deposit prompt).

**KYC:** deferred until first money movement. Lord depositing OR Vagrant cashing out → ID + selfie via IDV provider (Persona/Sumsub class) → states: unverified (watch/post only) → pending (can receive, can't withdraw) → verified (full rails) → flagged (Sheriff review). Show progress as "getting deputized" to keep the tone.

**Deposit (Lord):** amount picker ($100 min for title; smaller "Nickel Pouch" deposits allowed without title) → payment method → Monarch's 1% shown as the vulture taking his coin → 99 coins land with coin-rain → title ceremony (shareable card: "A new Baron rides into Perdition Gulch").

**Gift:** tap gift on any beg/stream → amount → preview the spectacle tier → confirm (Face ID above $100) → public animation fires → Vagrant notification with sound → both parties get a shareable receipt card. Sub-2-second happy path; every added tap costs whale revenue.

**Withdraw (Vagrant):** wallet → cash out → shows 10% Monarch cut itemized → linked bank/debit → risk holds surfaced honestly ("the Sheriff counts the gold for 24h on first cash-out") → paid → green cactus celebration.

**Beg:** composer → text + optional video → Sheriff pre-scan (pass/soft-flag/reject with reason) → optional AI Dramatize (runs *after* safety approval, per your doc — correct call) → preview as it appears in lobby → post (consumes 1 of 2 daily) → live state: views, reactions, gifts → 24h expiry → recap card.

**Challenge:** Lord composes (task + bounty + deadline) → Sheriff pre-scan (dangerous-acts filter is strictest here) → bounty escrowed → Vagrants accept → submit video proof → Lord verdict (approve/reject) → rejected proofs auto-route to dispute if the Vagrant appeals → payout or bounty return.

**Livestream:** Lord/verified Vagrant goes live → tipping rail + challenge overlay + guest seats → auto-clipper marks big-drop moments → clips flow to Feed of Madness.

**Report/Dispute/Appeal:** report (2 taps from anything) → Sheriff triage (AI severity sort) → human review for money-touching cases → outcomes logged on trust score → appeal at the Courthouse with 72h SLA. All money frozen, not seized, during disputes.

## STEP 9 — Gamification

- **XP** for every meaningful act (posting, gifting, receiving, streaming, dueling; weighted to *transactions* so XP correlates with GMV).
- **Levels 1–50 per class track:** Drifter → Beggar → Rascal → Notorious Outlaw → Folk Hero (Vagrant track); Squire → Lord → Baron → Tycoon → Monarch's Court (Lord track, gated by cumulative giving, not just deposits — pay-to-hold-title, give-to-climb).
- **Prestige:** at cap, reset to "Legend" variant with permanent gold-trim cosmetic. Prestige counts displayed on Wanted Posters.
- **Titles as purchases AND achievements:** base titles bought (revenue), rare titles earned only ("The Undertaker" — won 100 duels), legendary titles awarded one-per-season ("The Gold Rush King").
- **Badges:** common (streaks), rare ("Benevolent Baron" — gifted 50 different strangers), secret ("High Noon" — gave at exactly 12:00), seasonal-exclusive (scarcity).
- **Streaks:** daily login = coin dust (tiny amounts of unwithdrawable bonus currency — keeps free users in the economy without cash cost); gifting streaks for Lords (day 7 unlocks an exclusive animation).
- **Lucky Hours:** random 2x-visibility windows for begs, announced 10 min prior — appointment mechanics with slot-machine timing.
- **Daily/weekly/monthly:** daily roulette ticket, weekly "Bounty Board" quests, monthly headline event (Whale War Weekend, Beg-a-thon).
- **Seasons ("Gold Rushes"):** 10 weeks, themed cosmetics, leaderboard resets, season-end recap cards engineered for sharing, one legendary title per board.
- **Leaderboards:** daily/weekly/season × Richest Baron (given), Luckiest Drifter (received), Fastest Climber (class mobility), Most Ridiculous (community-voted). Rule of thumb: every persona must have a board they can plausibly top.

## STEP 10 — Community Systems

- **Follows → Fans → Worshippers:** three-tier relationship. Worshipper is opt-in and public ("kneel to a Lord") — deliberately absurd, deliberately screenshot-able. Lords can shower their Worshippers (your "narcissistic giving" idea — correct instinct; patronage is retention for both sides).
- **Courts:** a Lord's private lobby + chat with roles (Worshipper → Favored → Knight, appointed by the Lord). This is Discord-server belonging with a paymaster.
- **Public Saloons:** themed open lobbies (Comedy Saloon, Midnight Saloon, High Stakes Saloon) with their own regulars and culture.
- **Posses (guilds):** Vagrant teams for team begs and team leaderboards; Lord alliances for co-funded mega-challenges.
- **Events:** platform-run (Gold Rush kickoff), Lord-hosted (paid hosting slot — revenue), sponsored (brand bounties).
- **Comments & reactions:** reactions are themed (🤠💰🌵🔫→ replaced with a lasso icon for app-store safety) and *reactions are free spectacle; gifts are paid spectacle* — a clean upsell gradient.
- **Clips & sharing:** auto-clipper on big moments; every share carries watermark + referral code; referred signups earn coin dust (viral loop with an incentive, cheap because dust isn't withdrawable).
- **Recommendations/social graph:** follow graph + gift graph (who gave whom) powers the feed. The gift graph is your unique data asset — no other app knows who *pays* whom for fun.

## STEP 11 — Monetization (ranked by expected value)

1. **Transaction rake (1% Lords / 10% Vagrants ≈ 10.9% blended on GMV).** The engine. Keep it, disclose it loudly, make the vulture the mascot of it. Comparable platforms take far more (live-streaming platforms commonly take ~50% of gifting), so you have headroom — but see the Apple problem in Step 14 before promising these margins.
2. **Whale spectacle (premium gift animations).** Sell bigger *moments*, not bigger fees: the $50 "Stampede," the $500 "Gold Train," the $2,500 "Meteor of Generosity" that takes over every screen in the lobby. Pure margin on top of the rake.
3. **Monarch Pass (subscription, ~$15/mo Lords / ~$5/mo Vagrants).** Lords: fee rebates, exclusive animations, priority challenge placement, Court cosmetics. Vagrants: +1 daily beg, profile flair, early Lucky Hour alerts. Subscriptions smooth whale-lumpy revenue for investors.
4. **Cosmetics & titles.** Seasonal scarcity, rags-to-riches outfit ladders, character items. Steady Collector revenue.
5. **Lobby/event hosting fees.** Lords pay to host featured events; auction the Saturday-night headline slot.
6. **Sponsored begs & brand bounties.** "Wendy's put a $5,000 bounty on the best chicken-sandwich beg." High CPMs once the Feed of Madness has reach; sell after you have culture, not before.
7. **Season Pass** (battle-pass cosmetic track, $10/season).
8. **Future:** creator revenue share on clips, merch (the vulture will sell shirts), API/embeds for streamers, and — only with licenses — real-money games.

Deliberately excluded: interstitial ads (kills the vibe, pennies vs. the rake), selling user data (trust is the product).

## STEP 12 — Trust, Safety & Compliance

**Regulatory architecture (not a lawyer — build this WITH fintech + gaming counsel before writing code):**
- **Money transmission:** don't become the money transmitter yourself in v1. Partner with a licensed program manager / BaaS-payments provider (Stripe Connect-style custody, or an MSB sponsor) so funds flow through licensed rails while you operate the marketplace layer. Getting 40+ state MTLs yourself is a multi-year, multi-million-dollar project.
- **Gifting frame:** everything is a voluntary gift, no refunds, no goods/services exchanged, no loans — your doc already has this right. Challenges need care: "money for performing a task" edges toward payment-for-services; keep verdicts discretionary ("bounty granted at the Lord's pleasure") and run this specific mechanic past counsel.
- **Gambling:** no user-vs-user real-money wagering, no house-banked games, no paid roulette entry in v1. Beggar's Roulette = free entry, Lord-donated pots, sweepstakes rules (AMOE, official rules, state exclusions). Skill duels stake cosmetics/coin-dust only.
- **KYC/AML:** IDV at first money movement; sanctions screening; velocity rules; structuring detection (many small gifts aggregating to one cash-out is your #1 laundering pattern — score gift-graph anomalies); SAR process via your payments partner.
- **Tax:** 1099-K generation at IRS thresholds, in-app tax center, "gifts may be taxable" disclosures.
- **Age & geo:** 18+ hard gate verified at KYC; geo-fence prohibited states/countries at the payments layer, not just the app layer.

**The Sheriff system (layered):**
1. **Pre-publish AI scan** on all begs/challenges/streams-titles: text + video moderation APIs; hard-ban list (illegal acts, weapons, drugs, self-harm, sexual content, minors — zero tolerance with mandatory reporting, dangerous physical challenges, medical/emergency claims, hate); soft-flag queue for ambiguity.
2. **The desperation valve [STRATEGIC CHANGE]:** when AI detects *genuine crisis* begging (eviction, medical emergency, suicidal ideation), the correct move is not monetization — it's a gentle interstitial routing to real resources, and blocking the post from the comedy feed. This is both the ethical line and the thing that saves you when a journalist goes looking for "app profits from suicidal user." The brand is satire; the Sheriff knows when it stops being satire.
3. **Reputation:** 1–5 stars + trust score (account age, KYC tier, dispute history, report ratio) gating limits: new accounts get low caps that grow with tenure and clean history.
4. **Transaction safeguards:** 24h hold on first cash-out, cooling-off on gifts >$1,000 (with Face ID + "sleep on it" option), daily/weekly caps by trust tier, full audit ledger.
5. **Human review:** every money-touching dispute, every hard-flag, every big account action. Budget real moderation headcount from day one — AI triages, humans decide.
6. **Sheriff UI:** red warning badges, the "keep it legal & fun" composer banner with *examples of allowed wildness* (your instinct here was exactly right — showing what IS allowed shapes behavior better than listing bans).
7. **Responsible-spend tooling:** self-set deposit limits, self-exclusion, spend recaps. This is both ethics and app-store insurance.

## STEP 13 — Microinteractions

- **Taps:** everything money-related has weight — buttons depress with a leathery thunk; coins have physics (bounce, settle, one always rolls off).
- **Gifting:** tier-scaled. $1: single coin flip with a *ting*. $10: coin toss into a tin cup, metallic rattle. $100: gunshot + coin rain + screen flash. $500+: screen-shake, saloon doors burst open, the amount in wanted-poster type, every user in the lobby sees it. $2,500: the Gold Train rolls across the entire screen. Haptics scale with tiers.
- **Receiving:** the phone buzzes twice like a coin dropped in a cup; notification reads in-voice ("A Tycoon fancied your beg. 445 coins, partner."). Cash-out success: cactus blooms, green confetti — green ONLY here.

Master mute honored ruthlessly.

- **Streaks & rewards:** daily chest opens with a crowbar creak; streak flames are a lit dynamite fuse.
- **Empty/loading states:** wanted posters of the app's own mascots; loading spinner is a spinning revolver cylinder (review app-store gun-imagery tolerance; fallback: spinning lasso).
- **Notifications:** max 3/day default, each one written as a character line, never "You have a new notification." Notification copy is a content channel — treat it like a writers' room.

## STEP 14 — Critical Review (hostile-panel mode)

**Apple/Google:** *"This is a real-money app with gambling aesthetics, user-generated money requests, and 18+ content risk."* — Biggest single risk in the entire project. Three sub-problems: (a) **IAP economics**: if coins are bought in-app, Apple takes up to 30% of coin purchases, which vaporizes a ~11% take-rate business; you need external-purchase flows (US link-out entitlements post-Epic) or web-based deposits like Venmo-class fintech apps — this decision shapes the whole architecture, make it first. (b) **Review optics**: gun imagery, "begging," gambling-adjacent visuals invite rejection; keep a "clean build" review strategy and legal memo ready. (c) **Policy volatility**: one policy change can kill distribution — build web app parity from day one as a hedge.
**Sequoia/a16z:** *"Whale concentration + regulatory overhang = discounted multiple. But GMV take-rate on a new behavior with organic virality? We've funded worse."* They'll push on: cold start (chicken-and-egg between Lords and Vagrants) — answer: seed with 20 performer-Vagrants (comedians) and 5 house-funded Lord accounts running MrBeast-style drops for launch content; and CAC — answer: the product is the ad (clip loop), plus referral dust.
**YC:** *"Cut everything but the core loop. Ship begging + gifting + feed in 8 weeks. Everything else is Season 2."* Correct.
**Stripe/fintech expert:** *"Your fraud problem is triangulation: stolen cards → Lord accounts → gifts → mule Vagrants → cash-out. Model chargeback exposure NOW; 10% rake dies fast at 2% chargebacks."* Hence holds, caps, gift-graph anomaly scoring.
**The journalist you didn't invite:** *"App lets rich people make poor people dance for money."* Answers you must be able to give truthfully: the desperation valve exists, crisis begs are routed to help, everyone's in on the bit, Vagrants can become Lords, and top Vagrants out-earn entry Lords. If those aren't true, the story writes itself and it ends you.
**Biggest opportunities:** the gift graph (unique data), whale wars (proven revenue meta), class mobility (unique narrative), and the fact that nobody owns this category because everyone else is too scared of it — which is either your moat or the warning label.
